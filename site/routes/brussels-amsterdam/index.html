Brussels to Amsterdam by Train: Eurostar and Eurocity Direct Guide (2026)

Two direct trains link Brussels and Amsterdam. Eurostar takes about 1 hour 50 minutes and arrives at Amsterdam Centraal. Eurocity Direct runs every hour, takes about 2 hours and arrives at Amsterdam Zuid, with fares from about 25 EUR. This guide explains which to choose, how to buy tickets, what to know at the border, and what to do in Amsterdam.

  • Fastest time: About 1 h 50 min (Eurostar)
  • Trains a day: Eurocity Direct every hour, plus Eurostar
  • Prices from: About 25 EUR one way
  • Arrives at: Eurostar: Amsterdam Centraal. Eurocity Direct: Amsterdam Zuid.

Official sites: Eurostar Brussels to Amsterdam, Eurocity Direct (SNCB), NS International, Amsterdam visitor information

Fast facts and route overview

Two direct trains run between Brussels and Amsterdam. Eurostar is the faster one and ends at Amsterdam Centraal, in the middle of the city. Eurocity Direct (run by NS and SNCB) leaves every hour, is often cheaper, and ends at Amsterdam Zuid, a business district south of the centre. Both start at Brussels-Midi. Belgium and the Netherlands are both in the Schengen area.

Good to know: Which should you pick? Choose Eurostar for the fastest trip and a station in the centre of Amsterdam. Choose Eurocity Direct when it is cheaper or the time suits you better. Good to know: Works on some weekends change the routes: 14 to 15, 21 to 22 and 28 to 29 November and 6 to 7 December 2026. On 21 and 22 November Eurocity Direct runs to Amsterdam Centraal via Gouda and skips Schiphol. Check b-europe.com before you travel.

Which ticket to buy and what it costs

Eurostar and Eurocity Direct are separate trains with separate tickets. A ticket for one is not valid on the other. Buy early for the cheapest seats.

  • Where to buy: Eurostar tickets on eurostar.com. Eurocity Direct tickets on the NS International and SNCB (B-Europe) sites, and on Trainline.
  • How far ahead: Eurocity Direct tickets go on sale up to 335 days before travel. Eurostar tickets go on sale 7 to 12 months ahead, depending on the season.
  • Children: up to four children under 12 travel free with an adult on Eurocity Direct. For Eurostar, check its own child fares.
  • Bikes: Eurocity Direct takes bikes with a reservation that costs 13.90 EUR each, and space is limited.
  • Discount cards: some Belgian discount cards, such as Train+, apply to the Belgian part of a cross-border trip on regular trains, but not on high-speed trains.

Interrail and Eurail pass holders: check the pass rules and any reservation fees with the operator before you travel.

Timetable and journey times

Eurocity Direct runs 16 trains a day in each direction. From Amsterdam Zuid the first leaves at about 06:04, then trains leave hourly from 07:04 to 20:04, and a last one at 21:04 (June 2026 listing). Trains from Brussels run on a similar hourly pattern. Eurostar runs several times a day. Check the exact times for your date on the operators' sites.

Good to know: Eurocity Direct does not take seat reservations, so on busy trains you may have to stand or sit apart from your group. Eurostar guarantees a seat.

Stations and getting there

Brussels-Midi is a busy international station with metro lines 2 and 6 and pre-metro tram lines 3 and 4. Belgian stations use two languages, so Midi and Zuid are the same place. Eurostar passengers are asked to arrive at least 20 minutes before departure. Eurocity Direct has no check-in, so find your platform and board.

Amsterdam Centraal is the main station, in the city centre, with trams, metro, buses and ferries outside. Amsterdam Zuid is south of the centre. Metro line 52 and trains connect it to Amsterdam Centraal in about 10 minutes, and metro and train connections serve Schiphol Airport.

Amsterdam transport tickets: a GVB 1-hour ticket costs about 3.40 EUR and a 24-hour ticket about 10 EUR. Tap your card in and tap out when you leave, or the card charges a default fare.

  1. Check your station Look at your ticket. Eurostar goes to Amsterdam Centraal. Eurocity Direct goes to Amsterdam Zuid.
  2. Arrive on time For Eurostar, arrive at least 20 minutes before departure. For Eurocity Direct, 10 to 15 minutes is enough to find the platform.
  3. Find your platform Look for your train and platform on the board at Brussels-Midi. Platforms are shown shortly before departure.
  4. Keep your ID with you There are no passport queues, but customs and police do spot checks on this route.

On the train

  • Eurostar: a reserved seat. The luggage allowance in Standard and Plus is two pieces up to 75 x 53 x 30 cm and one small item, with no weight limit.
  • Eurocity Direct: open seating in 1st and 2nd class. There is no seat reservation, so board early if you want to sit together.
  • Bikes: Eurocity Direct takes bikes with a reservation (13.90 EUR each), and space is limited.
  • Food and facilities: check the operator pages for what is on board, and bring a snack and a drink for busy trips.

Belgium and the Netherlands are both in the Schengen area, so there is no passport control at Brussels-Midi, Amsterdam Centraal or Amsterdam Zuid. Eurostar says you do not need a passport for this trip, but customs officers carry out random spot checks, so bring an identity document.

Coach: FlixBus runs many buses a day. The fastest take about 2 hours 40 minutes, but many take longer in traffic. Fares start from about 10 EUR. In Brussels buses stop at Brussels-Nord and Brussels-Midi. In Amsterdam they use Amsterdam Sloterdijk and Amsterdam Centraal (De Ruijterkade).

Driving: the trip takes about 2.5 hours without traffic. Parking in central Amsterdam is expensive and the city restricts some vehicles. Most visitors leave the car outside the centre, or take the train.

Money-saving and first-timer tips

  • Compare both trains for your date. Eurostar is faster and arrives in the centre, but Eurocity Direct is often cheaper.
  • Check the arrival station before you book a hotel or onward trip. Amsterdam Centraal and Amsterdam Zuid are not the same place.
  • Book Eurocity Direct Saver early for the lowest fare, and remember it only works on the train you chose.
  • Travelling with children? Eurocity Direct is free for up to four children under 12 per adult.
  • In Amsterdam, never walk or stand in the bike lanes. Cyclists have priority.
  • Take your luggage into account. The Rijksmuseum does not accept large bags or suitcases, so leave them at your hotel or a luggage storage point.

Things to do in Amsterdam

Amsterdam is small and easy to walk, with trams and metro when you need them. Book the big museums before you leave home, because the best slots sell out. Prices are for adults, so check each official site first.

Anne Frank House

The hiding place where Anne Frank wrote her diary. Tickets are sold online only, on the official site. New tickets go on sale every Tuesday at 10:00 for visits six weeks later, and they sell out fast.

  • Time: About 1.5 hours
  • Cost: About 16.50 EUR for adults (check the official site)
  • From the station: About a 20-minute walk from Amsterdam Centraal, or tram 13 or 17 to Westermarkt.

Rijksmuseum

The national museum, with Rembrandt's Night Watch and Vermeer. A timed ticket is required, booked online, even if you have a museum card. Large bags and suitcases are not accepted, but there is a free cloakroom for small bags.

  • Time: 2 to 3 hours
  • Cost: 25 EUR for adults, rising to 26.50 EUR in 2027. Free for under 18s. Open daily 09:00 to 17:00.
  • From the station: Tram or a 20 to 30-minute walk from Amsterdam Centraal.

Van Gogh Museum

The largest collection of Van Gogh's paintings in the world. Timed tickets are sold online only, and they can sell out weeks ahead in busy months.

  • Time: 2 hours
  • Cost: About 27 EUR for adults (check the official site)
  • From the station: Next to the Rijksmuseum, on Museumplein.

Canals and the Jordaan

Walk the canal belt and the Jordaan neighbourhood for narrow streets, small shops and cafes. A one-hour canal cruise is the easiest way to see the centre from the water.

  • Time: 2 to 3 hours
  • Cost: Walking is free. Cruise prices vary by operator, so compare.
  • From the station: Start at Amsterdam Centraal or the Dam.

Vondelpark

The city's favourite park, with lawns, ponds and cafes. Good for a break between museums.

  • Time: 1 hour
  • Cost: Free
  • From the station: A short walk from the Rijksmuseum and the Van Gogh Museum.

Where to stay in Amsterdam

Amsterdam is a popular city break and beds fill up quickly, especially on weekends and in spring and summer. Book early, and check how far each place is from a tram or metro stop. The centre is small enough that most stays are a short ride from the sights.

Frequently asked questions

How long does the train from Brussels to Amsterdam take?

Eurostar takes about 1 hour 52 minutes. Eurocity Direct takes about 2 hours.

How much is a train from Brussels to Amsterdam?

Eurocity Direct Saver fares start from about 25 EUR in 2nd class. Eurostar starts from about 29 EUR. Flexible Eurocity Direct tickets cost about 65.80 EUR in 2nd class.

Which station do I arrive at in Amsterdam?

Eurostar arrives at Amsterdam Centraal, in the city centre. Eurocity Direct arrives at Amsterdam Zuid, and a metro or train takes about 10 minutes to Amsterdam Centraal.

Do I need to reserve a seat?

Eurostar tickets include a seat. Eurocity Direct has no seat reservations, so you sit in any free seat.

Do I need a passport?

There is no routine passport control, but customs and police do spot checks on this route. Carry your passport or ID card.

Is this guide also for Amsterdam to Brussels?

Yes. The same trains and fares run in both directions.

What if the train works change the route?

On some weekends Eurocity Direct runs a different route. Check b-europe.com before you travel.
